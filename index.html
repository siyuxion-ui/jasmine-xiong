Current Research

From Features to Workflows: How Creators Translate AI Capabilities for Everyday Audiences

Status. Sole-author working paper · Manuscript in preparation.

Research problem

As AI systems move from discrete features toward agents, skills, and multi-step workflows, technical capability does not automatically translate into user understanding or perceived value. This study examines how creators mediate that gap.

Research questions

  1. How do creators translate increasingly complex AI capabilities into accessible use cases?
  2. What communication strategies make AI capabilities understandable, useful, and actionable?
  3. What barriers to understanding, relevance, agency, trust, and potential adoption emerge in audience responses?

Data / Method

Douyin creator content + publicly visible audience responses. Systematic content analysis + thematic analysis.

Data collection in progress.

Emerging question. Whether creator-mediated AI communication simply helps users satisfy existing needs — or also helps them discover uses they had not previously articulated.

04About

Practitioner first, researcher by conviction.

Off duty — still observing

My path to human-AI research began in language and communication, moved through global brand marketing and creator practice, and eventually led me to a recurring question: why does technical capability so often fail to become meaningful human use?

Trained in language teaching (USC Rossier, MAT-TESOL), I treat adoption as a learning problem: people don't adopt what they can't understand — and understanding is built, message by message, by someone they trust. Years on the market side — campaigns, a 435K-follower creator account, co-founding AI Markethon — taught me how capabilities become relevance, relevance into trust, trust into use. Now I'm turning that practice into research.
